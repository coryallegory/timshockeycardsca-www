Privacy

Last updated October 6, 2026

This is an unofficial fan site. The stock map and the checklist work without an account, and we don't know who you are. If you create an account to track your collection, this page says what we keep, why, where, for how long, and how to delete it.

Without an account

  • No cookies from us. The site's pages set none. Cloudflare, which delivers the site, may set a security cookie of its own to tell people from bots.
  • Your location stays in your browser. If you allow it, your browser gives the map your location to centre it. It is never sent to us. The map remembers the last spot it found you, rounded to about 1 km, in your browser's storage (map.lastSpot), so it opens near you next time.
  • Map images come from OpenStreetMap (tile.openstreetmap.org), whose servers see your IP address and the part of the map you're looking at (their privacy policy). The Directions link opens Google Maps only if you choose it.
  • Visit counts, no ads. Cloudflare Web Analytics counts page views, without cookies or tracking you across sites.
  • Hosting. The pages are hosted on GitHub Pages behind Cloudflare, and the stock data comes from Amazon S3 through Cloudflare. Like any web host, these services see your IP address and the pages you request.

With an account: what we keep and why

Only what the account and collection features need:

  • Your email address, to sign you in and to send you a password-reset link when you ask for one. We send no other email: no sign-up confirmation, no newsletters.
  • Your password, as a hash only. We store a salted scrypt hash, from which the password can't be read back. The password itself is never stored or written to a log.
  • Your collection: the IDs of the cards you've ticked, how many copies of each, and the date each was first ticked.
  • Your trade profile: your trade id (one is chosen at random for you when you first open your account page; you can change it), and if you add them, your area (the first 3 characters of your postal code) and your external links (usernames on Instagram, X, Reddit, eBay or Facebook); whether it's public, and when you confirmed you're 18 or older.
  • Who you follow, if you follow traders, and when you started.
  • When the account was created and when its password last changed.
  • Your sign-ins (sessions): for each browser you're signed in on, a hash of its random session key, and when it started and expires.
  • Password-reset links you've asked for: a hash of each link's key and when it expires.

We don't ask for your name, and we don't use any of this for advertising or profiling.

Public trade profiles

  • Off until you turn it on. Your trade profile is private unless you switch it to public on your account page, and you can switch it off again at any time. Going public needs you to confirm you're 18 or older.
  • What a public profile shows, to anyone with the link, signed in or not: your trade id, your area (like "M5V area") if you added one, your collection's progress, your spares (extra copies) and wants (missing cards), and the external links you added, as links to those sites. Never your email.
  • Following is private. Nobody sees who you follow or who follows you, and the person you follow isn't told.
  • Nearby traders lets signed-in collectors find public profiles that have an area. Distances are between postal-area centres, rounded up to 5 km. You can search while your profile is private. If you choose Use my location, your browser finds the nearest postal area; only that area is sent for the search. Coordinates are never sent or saved.

Stopping abuse

To slow down password guessing and floods of requests, the server counts attempts: per IP address (sign-in, creating an account, forgotten and reset passwords), per email address (sign-in, forgotten password) and per account (changing the password, deleting the account, checking or claiming trade ids, and Nearby searches). Each count is stored with the IP address, email address or account ID it belongs to, and is deleted automatically once its 1-minute, 15-minute or 1-hour window is over (the database's automatic clean-up can take up to 2 days).

Server logs

The account server's logs are kept for 2 weeks. They record:

  • each request's type, address (such as which card was ticked or unticked, or which trade profile was opened, but not by whom), result and how long it took: no IP address, cookies, passwords or form contents;
  • your account ID when a password-reset email is sent or fails to send, and when an account is deleted;
  • details of errors, with email addresses removed;
  • the IP address of requests that try to reach the server directly instead of through api.timshockeycards.ca (these are refused).

Where it's kept

Account data lives with Amazon Web Services in the us-east-1 region (Northern Virginia, United States): the database (Amazon DynamoDB), the server (AWS Lambda) and the password-reset email (Amazon SES). Requests to the account server pass through Amazon CloudFront and its firewall, which see your IP address in order to block attacks.

Deleted account data is removed from the active database immediately after deletion succeeds. Recovery backups may retain it for up to 35 days; we don't use them to restore deleted accounts.

Deleting your account

On your account page (the round button with your initial at the top of any page, then Account settings), choose Delete account and enter your password. When deletion succeeds, your account, email address, collection, trade profile, follows, sign-ins and reset links are removed from the active database, and you're signed out everywhere. The email address can be used for a new account afterwards. Server log lines (2 weeks), abuse counters and recovery backups can remain for the periods above.

If deletion fails after it starts, your trade profile stays hidden and collection and profile edits are blocked. Sign in again and retry deleting your account to finish removing the remaining data.

Sharing

We don't sell your data or share it with anyone. The only other parties involved are the services that run the site, named above: Amazon Web Services, Cloudflare, GitHub and OpenStreetMap.
